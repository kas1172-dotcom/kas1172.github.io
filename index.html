Client-approved for production use

The problem. Manufacturers have useful information spread across customer records, orders, quotes, and public announcements. Those sources do not use the same names or identifiers. An opportunity is only useful if it can be connected to the right customer and the right capability.

What I built. Omni Prospect brings internal ERP, CRM, and quoting data together with government contract announcements and industry research. I own product design, architecture, development, and implementation with the client's technical and commercial teams.

The hardest part was the data. I designed a BigQuery gold layer with a consistent naming system for customers, sites, and business units, backed by explicit matching rules. A separate, deterministic scoring process ranks opportunities; LLM-assisted research explains the supporting evidence and leaves uncertain associations for human review.

Delivery. Built with React, TypeScript, FastAPI, PostgreSQL, and Docker, with deployment and data integration in the client's Google Cloud environment. Approved by the client for production use.

EMPATH Unit Clinical Staffing & Operations PlatformCoordinate staffing requests and track scheduling equity on a psychiatric unit.
Nurse SchedulingScheduling EquityOperational Analytics
Interactive demonstration · Synthetic unit data

The problem was on my own unit. Nurses were working schedules that left little room for a consistent life outside the hospital. Managers spent hours building schedules that still felt unfair, and the new unit needed a better way to show its staffing needs. I did not think that had to be accepted as normal.

So I built a tool. Nurses could submit preferences and see their schedules and equity scores. The scheduler considered unit coverage, call-offs, picked-up shifts, and past scheduling compromises. When someone received a less-than-ideal shift, that history informed the next schedule.

Making the case for staffing. A manager dashboard tracked census, length of stay, patient-level financial metrics, and nursing hours per patient. The data was incomplete, but the trends helped nurse management advocate for two additional nursing positions.

Public demonstration with fictional staff and synthetic aggregate unit totals. No patient records or hospital results are shown.

Live · Weekly

Working in healthcare compliance, I spent a disproportionate amount of time just staying current: scanning OIG enforcement actions, Federal Register notices, new CMS rules, FDA actions, and congressional activity across a dozen sources, for a dozen clients. The manual overhead was real. I'd spend hours each week sorting through noise to find the two or three things that actually mattered to a specific client in a specific regulatory context.

So I built infrastructure to do that automatically. A pipeline pulls from 30+ government APIs and industry feeds every Monday, uses a language model to classify urgency and synthesize the implications for two distinct audiences (compliance and strategy professionals, and policy readers) and publishes a structured briefing as a static site. Zero manual effort after setup. The dashboard I wished had existed when I needed it most.

Medicaid Financing WorkbenchExplore Medicaid financing through public federal datasets.
Python Claude API Public Federal Datasets Static Dashboard
Personal Project

A personal project I took on out of curiosity. The 2025 reconciliation law reshaped how Medicaid gets financed by phasing down the provider-tax ceiling, adding work requirements, and squeezing optional benefits. I wanted to understand which states were most exposed and by how much, so I set out to answer that question the way a research associate on a state-Medicaid policy team would: with the public federal datasets, not punditry.

The mechanical part of that work is the same grind every time, so I built an analyst workbench to automate it: fetch, clean, compute, chart, and draft. It ingests CMS-64, managed-care, Census coverage, and KFF policy data into one analysis-ready panel and runs four financing analyses: provider-tax cap exposure, an HCBS vulnerability index, work-requirements coverage loss, and dual-eligible spending concentration. A language model drafts each finding in a tell / show / so-what structure, and every draft ships with a claims-to-verify checklist, because the tool runs the arithmetic but the analyst owns every published conclusion.
